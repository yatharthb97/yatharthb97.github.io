**Annual Progress Report**

Over the past year, my work centred on the analysis of preliminary data, further optimisation of experimental protocols, acquisition of cell trajectories, and downstream data analysis.

The first part of the year was spent analysing previously acquired trajectories, which led to further optimisation of acquisition parameters to reduce errors from file compression and to minimise frame drops during video microscopy recording. This required significant modification of the control-layer codebase and computing infrastructure, as the volume of recorded data grew six-fold. Cell culture growth protocols were also revised to follow more standardised conditions, which in turn required upgrading incubator hardware to allow for air exchange during growth.

Further experiments investigated the sources of variability in cell swimming duration. I optimised protocols for measuring cell size distributions and established ensemble-level experiments. In the second half of the year, we renewed our cell lines and characterised swimming behaviour using both single-cell and ensemble approaches for an additional mutant line (ptx1).

We further developed and optimised surface treatment protocols for microfluidic devices to prevent cell adhesion, and extensively tested these protocols, since cell sticking would otherwise introduce a critical artefact into all downstream experiments.

A substantial part of the year was devoted to further develop a high-throughput, parallelised tracking pipeline, with efforts focused on maximising trajectory recovery; the current recovery rate is close to 99%. Considerable additional effort went into denoising and processing these trajectories, and into building an analysis pipeline to extract motility features from the resulting data. We also developed interactive tools to visualise and verify each step of this post-processing pipeline.

Two major infrastructural changes required adaptation of our working protocols and caused delays: relocation of the laboratory to another building, which required full reassembly of the microscopy setup, and closure of the microfluidics facility, which was addressed by outsourcing device fabrication to a facility at INESC, Lisbon. Both required additional optimisation work to maintain experimental continuity. In parallel, we began documenting the microscopy hardware, software, and other critical components of the setup; this documentation effort is ongoing, and an accompanying website is already live.

Severe delays in the renewal of my residency permit prevented me from attending any conferences in 2025. In 2026, I attended two workshops, one international conference, and a PhD meeting, presenting my work at each either as a poster or an oral presentation. I also hosted laboratory visits and discussed my work with several visiting scientists at the institute.
